## MD&A: Segment Performance

Intelligent Cloud revenue grew at a strong double-digit rate, led by Azure and other
cloud services, which continued to gain share as enterprise customers migrated
workloads and adopted AI-infused services. Productivity and Business Processes grew at
a more moderate rate, driven by Microsoft 365 commercial seat growth and price
increases. More Personal Computing grew modestly, with Windows OEM revenue roughly flat
against a soft PC hardware refresh cycle.

## MD&A: Capital Expenditures

Capital expenditures, including finance leases, increased substantially year over year
as the Company continued to invest in datacenter capacity to support cloud and AI
services demand. Management expects capital expenditures to remain elevated in the near
term as it builds out infrastructure ahead of anticipated demand, which will pressure
free cash flow margin even as operating margin remains strong.

## Risk Factors: AI Infrastructure Investment

The Company is making substantial investments in datacenters, networking, and
specialized hardware to support artificial intelligence services. These investments
require significant upfront capital and long lead times, and there is no assurance that
customer demand will materialize at a pace that generates an adequate return on this
capital within the expected timeframe. A slower-than-expected ramp in AI-related
revenue could pressure margins given the scale of committed infrastructure spend.

## Risk Factors: Cybersecurity

The Company's products and services, including its cloud offerings, have been and may
continue to be targeted by increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks, including
nation-state actors. A successful attack on the Company's systems or its customers'
data hosted on Microsoft platforms could damage the Company's reputation, result in
significant remediation costs, and expose the Company to regulatory investigations and
litigation.

## Risk Factors: Competition in Cloud Services

The cloud services market remains intensely competitive, with several large
well-capitalized competitors offering comparable infrastructure and platform services.
Continued price competition and the pace of feature innovation among competitors could
compress Intelligent Cloud segment margins if the Company is unable to maintain
differentiation through its AI and enterprise software integration strategy.
