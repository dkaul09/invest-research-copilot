## MD&A: Revenue Discussion

Revenue was roughly flat year over year on a reported basis, as growth in a small
number of geographies was offset by declines in Nike Direct digital sales and softness
in the Greater China market. Management has been actively reducing promotional
sneaker styles and classic franchises in the wholesale channel as part of a strategic
reset intended to improve full-price sell-through over the medium term, which weighed
on near-term wholesale revenue.

## MD&A: Margin Discussion

Gross margin expanded despite the revenue softness, reflecting lower freight and
logistics costs, a favorable channel mix, and fewer closeout markdowns compared to the
prior year, when the Company worked through elevated inventory levels. Operating
margin nonetheless declined year over year as the Company increased demand-creation
spending, including marketing tied to a renewed product innovation push, ahead of
expected revenue recovery.

## Risk Factors: China Market Exposure

Greater China represents a significant portion of the Company's revenue and brand
value, and the market has experienced softer consumer demand and increased competition
from domestic sportswear brands. Continued weakness in China consumer spending or
further share loss to local competitors could have an outsized impact on consolidated
revenue and brand halo effects in other markets.

## Risk Factors: Wholesale Channel Transition

The Company's strategy of reducing classic footwear franchises in the wholesale
channel while shifting investment toward innovation and direct channels carries
execution risk. If new product introductions do not resonate with consumers at a pace
sufficient to offset the deliberate reduction of legacy wholesale volume, near-term
revenue and operating leverage could be pressured for longer than currently
anticipated.

## Risk Factors: Input Costs and Foreign Exchange

The Company sources footwear and apparel production primarily from independent
contract manufacturers in Vietnam, China, and Indonesia. Fluctuations in labor costs,
raw material costs, and the U.S. dollar's strength relative to local currencies in
these sourcing countries can affect gross margin, particularly given the multi-quarter
lag between cost changes and pricing or hedging responses.
